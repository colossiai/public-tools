Here’s a **digest of Don M. McDonald’s book "Derivatives Markets"**, which is a widely used textbook for learning about derivative instruments such as futures, options, and swaps. The book is known for its clarity, practical approach, and balance of theory and real-world applications.

## **📘 Digest of "Derivatives Markets" by Robert L. McDonald**

### **1. Introduction to Derivatives**

- **Definition**: A derivative is a financial instrument whose value depends on the value of an underlying asset.

- **Types**:

  - **Forward contracts  **

  - **Futures contracts  **

  - **Options  **

  - **Swaps  **

- **Uses**:

  - **Hedging**: Protecting against price fluctuations.

  - **Speculation**: Betting on market directions.

  - **Arbitrage**: Profiting from price differences in different markets.

### **2. Forward and Futures Contracts**

- **Forward**: Customized contracts traded OTC (Over The Counter), not standardized.

- **Futures**: Standardized contracts traded on exchanges with daily margining and marking to market.

- **Pricing**:

  - Based on **cost-of-carry model**: Includes interest rates, storage, dividends, etc.

  - **No-arbitrage principle** ensures that mispricing opportunities are rare or temporary.

### **3. Options Markets**

- **Options**: Contracts giving the right (not obligation) to buy/sell at a strike price.

  - **Call option**: Right to buy.

  - **Put option**: Right to sell.

- **Key terms**: Intrinsic value, time value, in/out/at-the-money.

- **Payoff profiles** and strategies explained via graphs and equations.

### **4. Option Pricing Models**

- **Binomial Option Pricing Model**:

  - Discrete time model that approximates option price.

  - Uses **risk-neutral valuation**.

- **Black-Scholes Model**:

  - Continuous time model assuming lognormal asset price distribution.

  - Formula depends on volatility, time to maturity, risk-free rate, and strike price.

### **5. Greeks – Risk Sensitivities**

- **Delta**: Rate of change of option price with respect to underlying asset.

- **Gamma**: Rate of change of delta.

- **Theta**: Time decay of option price.

- **Vega**: Sensitivity to volatility.

- **Rho**: Sensitivity to interest rates.

- Used for **hedging** and managing portfolios.

### **6. Trading and Hedging Strategies**

- **Spreads**: Combining options to create payoff patterns (bull, bear, butterfly).

- **Straddles/Strangles**: Strategies to benefit from volatility.

- **Delta Hedging**: Using underlying assets to offset option position risk.

### **7. Swaps**

- **Interest Rate Swaps**: Exchange of fixed-for-floating interest payments.

- **Currency Swaps**: Exchange of principal and interest in different currencies.

- Valuation based on present value of expected cash flows.

### **8. Arbitrage and Derivatives Pricing**

- Pricing built on **law of one price** and **no-arbitrage conditions**.

- Tools like **replicating portfolios** and **risk-neutral valuation** are central.

### **9. Volatility and Implied Volatility**

- Historical vs implied volatility.

- **Volatility smile** and skew explained through market behavior and supply/demand.

### **10. Credit Derivatives and Exotic Options**

- **Credit default swaps (CDS)**.

- **Barrier options**, **Asian options**, and other non-plain-vanilla instruments.

- Overview of **structured products**.

### **11. Risk Management and Regulation**

- How firms use derivatives to manage risks.

- Overview of **Value-at-Risk (VaR)**.

- Regulatory considerations post-2008 financial crisis (e.g., Dodd-Frank).

## **🧠 Key Takeaways:**

- Derivatives are powerful tools but require precise understanding of pricing, risk, and market dynamics.

- No-arbitrage and replication are central to understanding derivatives valuation.

- Practical trading strategies and risk management techniques are deeply tied to derivatives theory.
