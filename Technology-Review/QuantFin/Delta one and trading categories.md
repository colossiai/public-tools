# What is delta one trading

Delta one trading refers to trading financial instruments that have a linear and direct relationship with the underlying asset's price movement. These instruments have a delta of approximately one, meaning their value changes almost one-for-one with the price of the underlying asset. Delta one products are often used in hedging, arbitrage, and speculative trading strategies.

### Key Features:

1.  **Linear Payoff**: Delta one products mirror the price movement of the underlying asset without leverage or optionality complexities.

2.  **Hedging and Arbitrage**: Often used by institutional traders for risk management or taking advantage of price discrepancies.

3.  **Low Cost**: Typically cheaper to trade than options or other derivative products due to their simplicity.

### Examples of Delta One Products:

- **Futures**: Contracts to buy or sell an asset at a predetermined price on a future date.

- **Forwards**: Similar to futures but are customized contracts traded over-the-counter (OTC).

- **Exchange-Traded Funds (ETFs)**: Funds that track the performance of an index or asset.

- **Swaps**: Agreements to exchange cash flows based on the price movement of an underlying asset.

- **Contract for Differences (CFDs)**: Financial derivatives allowing traders to speculate on price movements without owning the asset.

### Applications:

- **Hedging**: Mitigating exposure to price movements in the underlying asset.

- **Speculation**: Taking directional bets on asset prices.

- **Arbitrage**: Exploiting price inefficiencies between different markets or instruments.

- **Market Making**: Providing liquidity by quoting buy and sell prices in these instruments.

# Suggest other trading categories that not delta one

Trading categories outside of **delta one** involve financial instruments where the relationship between the price of the instrument and the underlying asset is **non-linear** or where the payoff depends on additional variables like time, volatility, or specific conditions. These categories include the following:

### **1. Options Trading**

- **Description**: Options are financial derivatives that give the buyer the right (but not the obligation) to buy or sell an asset at a predetermined price (strike price) before or at a specific date.

- **Key Characteristics**:

  - Non-linear payoff.

  - Sensitivity to multiple "Greeks" (e.g., delta, gamma, theta, vega).

  - Useful for strategies based on volatility, time decay, or price moves beyond a threshold.

- **Examples**:

  - Call and Put Options

  - Exotic Options (e.g., barrier, Asian, or binary options)

### **2. Volatility Trading**

- **Description**: Involves trading instruments that allow direct exposure to the volatility of an asset, rather than its price.

- **Key Characteristics**:

  - Commonly uses options or volatility indices like the VIX (Volatility Index).

  - Payoffs depend on changes in volatility rather than price movement.

- **Examples**:

  - Volatility futures and options

  - Variance swaps

### **3. Fixed-Income Derivatives**

- **Description**: Instruments derived from bonds, interest rates, or credit risk.

- **Key Characteristics**:

  - Non-linear sensitivities to interest rate changes.

  - Influenced by factors like duration, convexity, and credit spreads.

- **Examples**:

  - Interest rate options (caps, floors, swaptions)

  - Credit default swaps (CDS)

### **4. Structured Products**

- **Description**: Pre-packaged investment products that combine multiple derivatives and other instruments to achieve a specific payoff.

- **Key Characteristics**:

  - Highly customizable.

  - Often include optionality or conditional payoffs.

- **Examples**:

  - Equity-linked notes

  - Principal-protected notes

### **5. Convertible Bonds**

- **Description**: Bonds that can be converted into equity under specific conditions.

- **Key Characteristics**:

  - Payoff depends on both bond-like features (interest rate sensitivity) and option-like features (equity sensitivity).

- **Examples**:

  - Convertible debt issued by companies.

### **6. Leveraged and Inverse Products**

- **Description**: Instruments that amplify returns or move inversely to the underlying asset.

- **Key Characteristics**:

  - Non-linear due to the use of leverage or inverse mechanisms.

- **Examples**:

  - Leveraged ETFs

  - Inverse ETFs

### **7. Exotic Derivatives**

- **Description**: Non-standard derivatives with unique features and payoffs.

- **Key Characteristics**:

  - Complex pricing.

  - Tailored for specific investor needs or market conditions.

- **Examples**:

  - Quanto options (foreign exchange-sensitive options)

  - Cliquet options (periodic resets of strike prices)
