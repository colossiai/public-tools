# Why F stand for January, G stand for February etc in future month codes?

The assignment of letters to months in the futures market coding system, such as "F" for January, "G" for February, and so on, doesn't have a straightforward logical connection to the months themselves. Instead, the system was designed to be simple and efficient for traders and market participants to quickly reference future months and their contracts. Here's the reasoning behind it:

1.  **Avoiding Ambiguity**: The system uses single-letter codes to represent months, which keeps things short and simple. By using letters of the alphabet, it avoids confusion with numerical systems that could mix up month and year information.

2.  **Chronological Assignment**: The letter assignment is largely arbitrary but follows a chronological order, starting with "F" for January and continuing through the alphabet, skipping a few letters (e.g., "I" and "O") to avoid confusion with the number 1 and the letter "O" (which might look like zero). This creates the following sequence:

    - **F** = January

    - **G** = February

    - **H** = March

    - **J** = April

    - **K** = May

    - **M** = June

    - **N** = July

    - **Q** = August

    - **U** = September

    - **V** = October

    - **X** = November

    - **Z** = December

3.  **Historical Reasons**: The exact origin of this system isn't entirely clear, but it likely developed over time in the futures markets for its practicality. It is also believed to have evolved in the early days of commodity trading when using letter-based codes was more common and less prone to misinterpretation compared to numbers.

4.  **Avoiding Confusion**: By skipping "I" and "O", the system avoids potential confusion with numerals. For example, "I" might be confused with the number 1, and "O" with the number 0. This is a practical consideration for reducing mistakes in communication and transactions.

Thus, the codes are a product of historical practices and logistical convenience, designed to facilitate quick and error-free identification of futures contract months.
